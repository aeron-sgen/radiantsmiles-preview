Jawbone keeps its shape because a tooth root is pressing into it.

Take the tooth out and that job stops.

Our own bone grafting page reports that patients lose 40-60% of the bone surrounding an extraction site during the following three years. That page was captured on 30 July 2026.

You do not feel it happening. You notice it later, when a denture stops sitting the way it used to. Or when an implant plan comes back needing more work than expected.

This is the quiet part of losing a tooth. The gap is visible. The bone underneath it is not.

We image the site first.

A clinician examines the area and digital X-rays are taken. The question is simple: is there enough bone, and if not, how much is missing.

We tell you what the options are.

Sometimes a graft is placed at the same time the tooth is removed, which preserves the site. Sometimes it is a separate procedure later. Sometimes it is not needed.

  1. 01

    The graft is placed.

    The site is opened, the material is packed into the defect and the area is closed over. It is done under anaesthetic.

  2. 02

    You heal before anything is built on it.

    Grafted bone needs time to integrate before an implant goes in. Rushing that step is how implants fail.

  3. 03

    We re-check the site before the implant.

    New imaging confirms the bone is there. Only then does the implant get planned.

What does bone grafting cost?

We quote it after imaging, because the size of the defect decides the procedure. We do not publish treatment prices. A number given before we have looked would only change afterwards.

Will insurance cover it?

Coverage depends on your plan and on why the graft is being done. Delta Dental, Cigna, MetLife and the Culinary Health Fund are among the carriers listed on our insurance and financing page. Network status varies by office and by plan. Contact the office you want with your plan details before you book.

Does it hurt?

The site is fully numbed for the procedure. Soreness and swelling afterwards are normal and are managed with what we send you home with. Tell us if it goes beyond that.

Do I definitely need one?

Not necessarily. That is decided from the imaging, not from the fact that a tooth is missing. If you do not need a graft, we will say so.
